OFFICE MEMORANDUM 
 
Subject: Guidelines for preparation of Mining Plan and Mine Closure Plan for Coal and 
Lignite Mines, 2026. 
 
Chapter-I 
Preliminary 
 
1.1. Short title and commencement. - (a) These guidelines may be called the “Guidelines for 
preparation of Mining Plan and Mine Closure Plan for Coal and Lignite Mines, 2026”. 
(b) These Guidelines shall come into force 30 days from date of issue of these guidelines. 
Provided that the Mining Plans uploaded on SWCS portal (https://swcs.coal.gov.in) before 
coming into force of these guidelines shall continue to be governed by the guidelines dated 
31.01.2025.  
(c) The Mine Closure Plan, inclusive of the Progressive Mine Closure Plan and Final Mine 
Closure Plan, shall constitute an integral component of the Mining Plan. 
(d) “Guidelines for preparation and approval of Mining Plan for Coal and Lignite Blocks/Mines 
for Underground Gasification 2026” as amended from time to time shall be an integral part of 
this guideline.   
1.2. Objectives: (a) To optimize the extraction of coal and lignite resources that minimize waste 
generation while maximizing efficiency.  
(b) To emphasize the importance of systematic and scientific mining, to promote safety and 
health, to safeguard the interest of workers and local communities, to promote upgradation of 
infrastructure and adoption of global best technologies. 
(c) To promote responsible mining practices that support both the coal and lignite industry and 
the broader ecosystem by incorporating restoration, remediation, and regeneration measures 
into mining plans for sustainable management of natural resources.  
(d) To prioritize environmental conservation by monitoring soil, groundwater, and vegetation 
and implementing measures to improve air, soil and water quality.  
1.3. Definitions: In these guidelines, unless the context otherwise requires: 
(a) “Abandoned Mine” means such mine as has been abandoned with no intention of working in 
the future; 
(b) “Discontinued Mine” means such mine as has been discontinued for any reason and are 
inaccessible or rendered inaccessible but are likely to be worked again.

P a g e 4 | 46 
 
(c) “Escrow account” means an account opened by the project proponent in a scheduled 
commercial bank, facilitating the deposition and withdrawal of funds for mine closure 
purposes; 
(d) “First-mile connectivity” means seamless movement of coal from the mine to the dispatch point 
with minimum road transport and mechanized loading onto railway rakes. It shall also include 
direct coal transfer to the consumer through conveyors, rails or any other mechanized means; 
(e) “Leased Area” shall have the same meaning as defined in Section 3 (ac) of MMDR, Act, 1957; 
(f) “Life of the Mine” means the period starting from the date of receiving mine opening 
permission and till end of post closure monitoring period; 
(g) “Mine closure” means the comprehensive process of closing and securing a mine during 
development and production period and upon completion of post closure monitoring period;  
(h) “Mine Closure Plan” means a document that includes financial provisions and activities to be 
implemented towards mine closure during development and production period and till final 
mine closure; 
(i) “Minor Changes” shall have the same meaning as prescribed in sub-rule (2) of Rule 22-E of 
the Mineral Concession Rules, 1960 as amended from time to time; 
(j) “Post closure monitoring period” means the period of 2 (two) years after completion of the post 
mining closure period; 
(k)  “Post mining closure period” means the period of 3 (three) years after cessation of the 
production activities; 
(l) “Production Life of the Mine” means the period of development and production years as given 
in the calendar plan; and  
(m) “Project Area” means the total area of the mine (as defined in the Occupational Safety, Health 
and Working Conditions, Code, 2020) specified in the mining plan and includes the area 
required for overburden dumping and associated infrastructure such as office premises, 
residential areas, R&R colonies, outside the mining lease area;   
1.4. Requirement and Applicability:   
(a) These guidelines supersede all previous guidelines including “Guidelines for preparation of 
Mining Plan and Mine Closure Plan for Coal and Lignite blocks, 2025” dated 31.01.2025. 
Provided that the “Guidelines for the Management of discontinued/abandoned/closed mines 
before the year 2009” dated 28.10.2022 shall continue to remain valid.   
(b) Every coal or lignite mine shall have a Mining Plan approved by the Coal Controller based on 
which mining operations shall be carried out .  
Provided that the Mining Plan of coal/lignite mines of Central Public Sector Undertakings, the 
State Public Sector Undertakings, Damodar Valley Corporation and SCCL, when the same is 
prepared by CMPDIL, may be approved by the respective Board. 
Provided further that Mining plans not required to be approved by the Coal Controller, shall be 
uploaded on SWCS portal within one month of approval by respective Boards. 
(c) No coal or lignite block shall be operational without an approved Mining Plan. 
(d) The approved Mining Plan shall be valid for the life of the mine.

Provided that the competent authority may approve any revision(s) or modification(s) of the 
Mining Plan, and upon such approval, the revised or modified Mining Plan, as the case may 
be, shall remain valid for remaining part of the life of the mine. 
(e) These guidelines shall apply to all coal and lignite mines including sand mining for use of 
stowing in mines and Mining Plans for sand mining for the cited use shall be prepared in 
accordance with the norms prescribed by the respective states.  
Provided that in the event that a state has not formulated such norms, a sample format for

(f) All abandoned mines and discontinued mines since 27th August 2009 shall achieve mine

(g) All discontinued mines, abandoned mines and closed mines upto 26th August 2009 shall

discontinued/abandoned/closed mines before the year 2009” dated 28.10.2022.  
(h) All Mining Plans approved under provisions of previous guidelines shall be deemed to have 
been approved under these guidelines.  
Provided that any modification or revision in approved Mining Plans shall be in accordance 
with provisions of these guidelines. 
(i) A Mining Plan may be formulated for multiple leased areas, or a leased area may have multiple 
Mining Plans. 
Chapter-II 
Mining Plan (excluding mine closure plan) 
2.1. The Mining Plan shall be formulated as per approved Geological Report. The geological report 
shall take into account the prevailing ISP norms and guidelines/ directives issued by the Central 
Government.  
2.2. Components of Mining Plan:  
2.2.1. General: 
(a) The Mining Plan will take into account the project's location, detail of allotment or allocation, 
previous approval of the Mining Plan (if any), surface features of the block/mine, brief socio-
economic information.   
(b) In case of unexplored/ partially explored blocks, the project proponent shall decide the 
appropriate Mining Method (Opencast or Underground or Mixed Mining and/or Underground 
Coal Gasification) and suitable technology to carry out mining operations so as to achieve 
optimum extraction of coal or lignite while ensuring safe and sustainable resource 
management. The project proponent may provide an analysis as regards arriving on appropriate 
mining method.  
(c) Mining Plan shall contain year-wise calendar plan and indicative production of coal or lignite 
as well as removal of overburden along with stage plan for critical years such as 1st year, 5th 

--- PAGE 8 ---
 
P a g e 6 | 46 
 
year, the year of achieving rated capacity of the mine, 10th year, 30th year and the final year 
(i.e. at the end of mine life). 
Provided that a block/mine may increase annual coal production upto rated capacity ahead of 
the schedule, irrespective of approved calendar plan. 
Provided further that a block/mine may modify the annual coal production downwards with 
prior recommendation of competent authority, by providing proper justification. 
Provided also that the Mining Plan shall include the action plan for the unexplored part of the 
block, if any, and liquidation of remaining resources to be projectized. 
(d) The project area envisaged in the Mining Plan must be restricted to the allotted or vested 
geological block boundary or existing mining lease. A certificate to this effect shall be provided 
by the Qualified Person (QP) or accredited Mining Plan Preparing Agency (MPPA) that has 
prepared the Mining Plan.  
Provided that with permission of the concerned land owner (Central Government or State 
Government or an individual or any other entity), the project proponent may carry out its 
activities beyond the allotted or vested geological block boundary or existing mining lease. 
(e) If any part of an allocated/vested block area cannot be included in the Mining Plan due to 
constraints such as presence of dense forest (in terms of the Forest Survey of India), dense 
habitation, rivers, railway lines and other areas that require special permission, such area may 
be excluded from the Mining Plan. 
Provided that in case such constraints are removed subsequently, a revised Mining Plan may 
be submitted for approval. 
2.2.2. Safety 
The project proponent shall ensure that due consideration is paid to safety aspects while 
preparing and submitting the Mining Plan. 
2.2.3. Mechanization:  
(a) All mines having peak rated capacity of 2 MTPA or more, shall ensure evacuation of 
coal/lignite through means of transport other than road, within 5 years of commencement of 
coal production, in case distance between mine and railway siding/ end user is less than 25 km. 
Provided that in case transportation by road is not avoidable or the distance is more than 25 km 
and the same is certified by the concerned District Collector(s), the project proponent may 
construct dedicated road with minimum involvement of public road.  
(b) All mines shall ensure that the loading infrastructure is mechanized within 5 years of 
commencement of coal production. 
The project proponent may choose mining method involving drilling and blasting in coal 
extraction (excluding overburden removal) only when blast-free technology is not technically 
feasible or is commercially unviable and shall provide justification for the same. 
2.3. Annexures: Along with the Mining Plan, the project proponent shall submit the following 
documents (as annexures) at the time of submission of the application on the portal: 
(a) Copy of allotment letter or ownership record. 
(b) Copy of approval of Board of the company towards submission of Mining Plan.

Chapter-III 
Mine Closure Plans 
3.1. Mine Closure Plan aims to ensure scientific mine closure and rehabilitation of mining sites, 
minimising environmental degradation, safeguarding public health, and promoting sustainable 
development by restoring the land to a condition suitable for future use or returning it to its 
near-original state or better, while also holding project proponents accountable for their closure 
obligations.  
3.2. Mine closure includes activities such as replanting native or suitable vegetation, restoring water 
bodies, rehabilitating wildlife habitats and activities related to agriculture, pisciculture, eco-
park, recreational, landscaping, waterbody conservation or creation as per Mission Amrit 
Sarovar, irrigation, solar, green energy and green industries including micro, small and medium 
enterprises. 
3.3. Backfilling of mine void may form part of mine closure activity only where it is carried out 
specifically for the purpose of mine closure and not as part of regular mining operation. 
3.4. Activities undertaken towards just transformation may be audited by third-party agencies 
appointed by the Central Government (list available at clause 3.14 (vi)). 
3.5. Mine Closure Plan shall comprise of (a) Progressive Mine Closure Plan, updated every five 
years, and (b) Final Mine Closure Plan. 
Provided that five years before cessation of mining operations, the project proponent shall 
submit a revised final mine closure plan that shall include operational history of the mine, 
scientific findings, maps/drawings, monitoring data and expenditure from the escrow account. 
Provided further that while submitting the revised Final Mine Closure Plan, the project 
proponent shall submit details of the updated cost estimate for various mine closure activities 
and balance amount in the escrow account. 
3.6. The Action plan for carrying out all mine closure operations (progressive mine closure as well 
as final mine closure) should be furnished in the format specified in Appendix-III. 
3.7. The Final Mine Closure Plan starts towards end of mine life and covers restoring land to its 
near-original state, repurposing and community development. It includes closure of mine, 

--- PAGE 10 ---
 
P a g e 8 | 46 
 
dismantling and demolition of structures, removal of machinery, plantation, landscaping, 
specified post-environmental monitoring, protective and restoration measures, barbed wire 
fencing, and related activities. 
3.8. Final Mine Closure Plan shall contain a completion report containing operational history, past 
investigation/study/scientific findings and further action taken, if any; mine working maps, 
images and drawings; mine water discharge and their uses, GPS coordinates for all salient 
features, safely accessible open portions of mine workings, dumps, area depillared, partially 
extracted and standing on pillars. 
3.9. The Final Mine Closure shall end with post closure monitoring period.  
3.10. Surrender of mine after partial extraction:  
(a) In case of surrender of mine after partial extraction, the CMPDIL shall, within a period of 45 
days from receiving intent of such surrender, prepare a temporary mine closure plan for which 
the cost shall be borne by Project Proponent.  
(b) The purpose of such temporary mine closure plan is limited to securing the exposed and mined 
area from safety point of view taking into account the already approved progressive mine 
closure plan. 
(c) The project proponent shall carry out activities mentioned in the Temporary Mine Closure Plan 
prepared by CMPDIL. For this purpose, the project proponent shall not be allowed to utilize 
the amount available in the escrow account.  
(d) The project proponent shall be the custodian of the mine until it is reopened or for a period of 
five (5) years, whichever is earlier.  
(e) If the mine is not allotted within five (5) years, final mine closure activities shall be undertaken 
by CMPDIL utilizing the amount available in the escrow account.  
3.11. Components of Mine Closure Plan: 
3.11.1. A zone of impact should be identified in the project area and buffer zone of 10 km from project 
boundary to address livelihood and income effects due to mine closure. 
3.11.2. Project proponent shall engage local communities and self-help groups through structured 
consultations and collaborative decision-making to incorporate their perspectives and develop 
sustainable solutions.  
3.11.3. After the closure of mining operations, all social amenities and infrastructure created like 
hospitals, schools, community centers, office and other buildings should be handed over to the 
Government or local authorities in consultation with district collector.  
3.12. Mine Closure Cost: The project proponent shall deposit amount in the escrow account towards 
mine closure cost as per calculation indicated at 3.13. The project proponent shall indicate 
breakup of cost towards individual activities (Appendix--III) of progressive mine closure as 
well as final mine closure.  
3.13. Escrow Amount: 
(a) The Project Proponent shall open an escrow account in a scheduled commercial bank followed 
by a tripartite agreement involving CCO before starting mining operations and shall deposit its 
contribution towards mine closure for that financial year.

(b) The annual rate for deposition towards the escrow account is ₹ 14,00,000 (Fourteen Lakhs per 
hectare) for opencast mines and ₹ 2,00,000 (Two Lakhs per hectare) for underground mines.

(d) The amount as calculated at clause 3.13 (c) shall be compounded @5% annually.

(e) The project proponent shall deposit the annual amount for 2nd year onwards in the escrow

Ministry of Coal, DGMS, MoEF&CC and the respective State Government.  
Provided also that once the dues are paid, the suspension order shall be revoked and mining

Provided also that upon lapse of six months after the suspension order, if project proponent 
fails to clear the dues, the Central Government may, on recommendation of the Coal Controller, 
withdraw approval of the Mining Plan. 
3.14. Reimbursement from Escrow Account:  
(i) Claim for reimbursement from the escrow account may be made by the project proponent up 
to 50% of the total deposited amount in the previous year (excluding interest accrued) based 
on work done towards progressive mine closure.  
(ii) Such claim shall be based on self-certification by authorized official of the company and shall 
be submitted on or before 31st March of subsequent financial year.   
(iii) While submitting the claim, videos and georeferenced images of the work done shall be 
submitted to the office of the Coal Controller for records.

3.15. Mine Closure Certificate: CCO shall issue the mine closure certificate after the post-closure

Chapter-IV 
Formulation, submission, scrutiny, approval, revision, and timelines   
4.1. Project Proponent shall ensure that the Mining Plan has been prepared by the Qualified Person/ 
Accredited Mining Plan Preparing Agency recognised under section 22B of the Mineral 
Concessions Rules, 1960, as amended from time to time. 
4.2. Every Mining Plan submitted for approval or revision shall be accompanied by a non-
refundable application fee of ₹ 1,00,000 (One Lakh Rupees), which may be revised by the 
Central Government from time to time.  
4.3. Vide notification no. CG-DL-E-01062020-219668 dated 29th May 2020 of the Ministry of 
Coal, the Coal Controller has been authorised to approve the Mining Plan under sub-section 
(1) of section 26 read with clause (b) of sub-section (2) of section 5 of the Mines and Minerals 
(Development and Regulation) Act, 1957 (67 of 1957).  
4.4. For carrying out its functions as required under clause 4.3, the Coal Controller shall be assisted 
by a Technical Committee that shall scrutinise and examine the Mining Plan submitted by the 
project proponent. After the scrutiny and examination, the committee shall recommend the 
Mining Plan to the Coal Controller for "Approval" or "Rejection".  
4.5. The Technical Committee shall consist of:  
a) Deputy Coal Controller - Member Secretary (In case of vacancy, the Coal Controller 
may designate a suitable officer)  
b) Director (Technical), MPS, Ministry of Coal- Member 
c) One officer nominated by Coal Controller. No officer shall serve as member of the 
committee for more than a year- Member 
d) An officer not below the rank of General Manager from CMPDIL (nominated by CMD, 
CMPDIL) in case Mining Plan is not prepared by CMPDIL. No officer shall serve as 
member of the committee for more than a year- Member 
4.6. The members of the Technical Committee shall scrutinise the Mining Plan and submit 
comments thereof within fifteen (15) days of submission of the Mining Plan on the portal. Non-
submission of comments by a member within such period shall imply that the member has no 
comments to offer.  

--- PAGE 14 ---
 
P a g e 12 | 46 
 
4.7. On receipt of the comments, the project proponent may consider incorporating the same in the 
Mining Plan and shall resubmit the modified Mining Plan, within a period of 30 days of expiry 
of the period mentioned in clause 4.6. 
Provided that in case the modified Mining Plan, considering the suggestions of members of the 
Technical Committee, is not resubmitted within such period, the Technical Committee may 
recommend rejection of the application. 
Provided further that an opportunity of being heard shall be given to Project Proponent before 
rejecting application for approval of Mining Plan.  
4.8. After the modified Mining Plan has been submitted, a meeting of the Technical Committee 
shall be convened within 15 days of submission under clause 4.7, wherein the project proponent 
shall make a presentation before the Technical Committee.     
4.9. Technical Committee shall circulate the approved minutes within 7 days of the meeting under 
clause 4.8.  
4.10. The Project Proponent shall incorporate decisions communicated as per minutes of the meeting 
of Technical Committee and resubmit the Mining Plan within 15 days of issue of minutes of 
the meeting. 
4.11. After final submission of the Mining Plan, a virtual/physical meeting of the Technical 
Committee may be convened to recommend the approval or rejection of the Mining Plan, 
taking into consideration the recommendations contained in minutes of the meeting. 
4.12. In case of recommendation for rejection, the Technical Committee shall accord the project 
proponent an opportunity of being heard before its recommendation. The Technical Committee 
shall record reasons for the rejection. 
4.13. After the Technical Committee recommends approval/rejection of the Mining Plan, the Coal 
Controller shall communicate its decision within 5 days of such recommendation.  
4.14. Circulation of Approved Mining Plan: CCO shall circulate a soft copy of the approved 
Mining Plan along with the approval letter to the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate 
Change, DGMS, CGWB, State Directorate of Geology and Mining department and District 
Administration. 
4.15. Any person aggrieved by any orders made or directions issued in respect of the Mining Plan 
by the Coal Controller may apply to the Secretary, Ministry of Coal, for revision of such order 
made or directions issued thereon. 
Provided that such application shall be made within a period of 30 (thirty) days of such order 
made or such direction issued.

(b) Revisions (other than minor changes), based on the application in this regard by the 
project proponent, shall be done only after approval of the Coal Controller. 
Provided that no revisions shall be approved by the Coal Controller without prior 
approval of the Nominated Authority if such revisions result in changes in the terms & 
conditions or efficiency parameters mentioned in CMDPA/CBDPA/LBDPA. 
(c) An application for Revision of the Mining Plan shall be accompanied by an undertaking 
to the effect that there have been no violations during the course of mining operations 
as regards safety, environment and lease-related matters.  
Provided that in case of violations related to safety (as reported by DGMS), leased area 
(as reported by State Government authorities), Environment (as reported by 
Central/State environment authorities), the Project Proponent shall ensure that 
mitigative measures for such violations are proposed by the project proponent in the 
application.  
Provided further that the Coal Controller or the Technical Committee may require the 
Project Proponent to make a presentation explaining the Revisions. 
(d) Notwithstanding anything contained in clause 5.1. (a), (b) & (c), the Coal Controller 
may modify or revise Mining Plan based on directions issued by the central 
government. 
5.2. 
A report shall be submitted by the project proponent with respect to compliance with the 
Mining Plan, including deviations, if any, annually to the Coal Controller.  
5.3. 
Through a joint application of project proponents of adjoining mines, a proposal may be 
submitted for extraction of barrier coal.  
5.4. 
Through mutual agreement, one mine may utilize void/ land of some other mine for purpose 
of handling overburden, subject to requisite approvals/clearances. 
5.5. 
Mine Repurposing Advisory Committee: A Mine Repurposing Advisory Committee will be 
set-up in every district where mining operations are carried out in order to oversee 
implementation of community development and livelihood plan. The Committee shall consist 
of: 
a) District Collector, or District officer (as authorised by D.C.) - Chairperson  
b) Sarpanch/Secretary of Local Gram panchayat – Member  
c) Representative of reputed NGO (in consultation with D.C.) – Member  
d) Nodal Officer (Mine Closure) of concerned coal mining company – Member  
e) Agent of the mine – Member 
f) Chairperson may nominate two other members, one of which shall be a forest official. 
Roles of Committee: 
a) Facilitating periodical meetings and open forums to collaboratively envision and co-
create sustainable ideas, consultations, and collaborative decision making.

b) Promoting initiatives that build local knowledge, skills, and resources, such as training 
programs or alternative livelihood projects, to help the community transition 
successfully post-mining.  
c) Ensuring engagement of local communities and self-help groups in Operation & 
Maintenance of the project and public spaces.  
d) Ensuring greening of project area through best suited species of plantation and 
sustainable tourism practices by promoting Geo-Tourism.  
e) Proactive participation of local community in the domain of selecting skills to be 
developed to generate alternate economic resources to ensure ‘just-transition’. 
f) Promote transparency and accountability in community-driven initiatives. 
5.6. 
The district administration may create market mapping of skills, merge income-generating 
schemes and utilise the District Mineral Fund (DMF). 
5.7. 
Though not mandatory, the project proponent may incorporate the following in the Mining 
Plan: 
(a) Make in India: Place a strong emphasis on sourcing indigenous machinery or equipment 
featuring locally manufactured components.  
(b) Green Energy and Emission Reduction: Actively strive to meet emission reduction in annual 
greenhouse gas emissions through renewable sources.   
(c) Clean Mobility in Mining Operations: Actively promote deployment of electric vehicles (EVs) 
and gas-based vehicles within coal mine operations.  
(d) Automation: Look toward the future of mining by exploring and integrating autonomous Heavy 
Earth Moving Machinery (HEMM) within their sites. 
(e) Use of blast-free mining technologies (such as longwall/continuous miner) in underground 
mines.

Removal of difficulties: Interpretation of the Ministry of Coal as regards provisions of the

5.9. 
In the interest of the development of the coal or lignite blocks, the Ministry of Coal may relax 
or modify any of the provisions of these guidelines. 
5.10. In the event of any inconsistency or ambiguity between the provisions of these Guidelines and 
the Appendices annexed hereto, the provisions of these Guidelines shall prevail and shall be 
binding to the extent of such inconsistency or ambiguity. 
5.11. These Guidelines are without any prejudice to any other relevant rules and regulations issued 
by concerned government authorities.
